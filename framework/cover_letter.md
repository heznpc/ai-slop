I am writing to submit the manuscript "Beyond Video Slop: A Multi-Format Framework for Understanding AI-Generated Content Pollution" for consideration as an Original Research Article in Big Data & Society.

This paper addresses a timely gap in the literature on AI-generated content. While discourse on "AI slop" has focused overwhelmingly on video -- driven by the Sora shutdown (March 24, 2026), YouTube's anti-slop campaign, and Instagram's pivot toward authenticity -- the paper demonstrates that video is among the least threatening formats. Text, fake reviews, and hybrid image-text formats such as card news score substantially higher on threat metrics yet receive a fraction of the research attention.

The manuscript makes three core contributions. First, it proposes the AI-Generated Multi-Format Slop Model (AMSM), a five-dimensional framework enabling systematic comparison of AI slop across eight content formats. Second, it formalizes the Production-Detection Paradox, demonstrating that research investment is inversely correlated with actual threat level. Third, it introduces the Algorithm-Slop Co-evolution Model, which reconceptualizes platform algorithms not as neutral distribution channels but as active participants in a four-stage feedback loop with slop production.

This manuscript is well suited to Big Data & Society. It is explicitly interdisciplinary, integrating computer science (detection benchmarks, adversarial robustness), social science (platform dynamics, algorithmic amplification), and policy analysis (regulatory gaps across the US, EU, Korea, and China). It is framework-oriented, aligning with the journal's tradition of conceptual contributions that open new research directions. It is also directly policy-relevant, with implications for AI labeling mandates currently being implemented across multiple jurisdictions.

Importantly, the paper goes beyond theory. It proposes seven testable hypotheses with specified methods, feasibility assessments, and impact ratings -- several leveraging natural experiments in the 2025-2026 period that create a time-limited window for empirical validation.

I should note that this manuscript is authored by an independent developer and researcher, not from within a traditional academic institution. This practitioner perspective informs the production cost analyses and automation pipeline assessments throughout the paper, offering granularity that complements institutional research. Where practitioner knowledge is used, it is clearly identified.

The manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration at any other journal. It contains approximately 9,500 words (excluding references), within the journal's guidelines for Original Research Articles.
