1.
AI failure isn't always a crash.
Sometimes the system keeps running, keeps sounding confident — and nobody notices it has quietly become something else.
The scariest failure mode is invisible.
That's the problem researchers are finally giving precise language to.

2.
Think of an AI's behavior as a landscape.
Stable outputs sit in valleys. Ridges mark where the system's character shifts.
This geometric framing changes everything — the question stops being "is this model good?" and becomes: where are the valleys moving?

3.
Prompt engineering moves you around inside a valley.
Retraining reshapes the entire landscape.
When valleys move, the invisible rules — what the system won't say, when it hedges, where it defers — can shift with zero visible signal.
They call this Silent Authority Transfer.

4.
There's also a recovery-time test.
Stress the system deliberately. Measure how long it takes to return to baseline.
Mathematically, that's identical to the Lyapunov exponent in dynamical systems.
Recovery speed and structural stability turn out to be the same property, measured two different ways.

5.
Both signals combine into a composite Trust Index.
Built for deployment, not just research papers.
For the first time, there's a precise answer to: has this AI quietly stopped being what it was?
