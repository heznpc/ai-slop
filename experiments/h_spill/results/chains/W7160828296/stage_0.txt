Insider threats represent a major cybersecurity challenge, particularly in financial environments where malicious activities are performed using legitimate credentials. Traditional anomaly detection approaches often fail to capture the relational and temporal dependencies present in authentication logs, especially under severe class imbalance. This paper proposes a hybrid GATv2-LSTM-GAN architecture for graph-based insider threat detection. The model combines GATv2 for relational representation learning, LSTM for temporal behavioral modeling, and GAN-based synthetic anomaly generation with Focal Loss to mitigate imbalance. The approach was evaluated on a synthetic dataset containing approximately 17.8 million authentication events with only 1,514 anomalies. Results demonstrate that the proposed architecture achieved robust performance under highly imbalanced conditions, outperforming the standalone Bi-LSTM baseline in terms of MCC while maintaining balanced Precision and Recall. The findings also highlight the limitations of relying exclusively on ROC-AUC in rare-event cybersecurity scenarios and reinforce the importance of imbalance-aware evaluation metrics such as MCC and PR-AUC.
