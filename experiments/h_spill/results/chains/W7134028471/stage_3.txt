1.
The robot you picture — chrome body, blinking eyes — is already obsolete. Today a robot is a flood-zone drone, a soft sleeve crawling through rubble, an AI negotiating your refund. Same word, sixty years, completely different thing. Our mental model hasn't caught up. That gap is a design problem.

2.
When a category fractures, its assumptions fracture with it. "Robot" used to mean: machine over here, human over there. Now a garment that reads your heartbeat is robotic. So is an agent that learns your habits and nudges your choices. Designing them like the old model is a mistake.

3.
The serious thinking isn't happening in mechanical engineering labs. It's in HCI — human-computer interaction research. HCI always asked what robotics skipped: not just *does it work*, but *how does it feel*? Do you trust it? Would you let it into your home? Those used to be soft questions. Now they're the hard ones.

4.
"Relational robotics" is the frame worth watching. The argument: what defines a robot isn't actuators or autonomy — it's the character of its relationship with a person. That reframe changes everything. The question stops being *what can it do* and starts being *what kind of relationship are we building*?

5.
Whoever figures out the interaction design will shape these systems more than whoever builds the hardware. The interface is not decoration — it's the argument the machine is making about its relationship to you. Get that wrong and the engineering underneath it doesn't matter.
