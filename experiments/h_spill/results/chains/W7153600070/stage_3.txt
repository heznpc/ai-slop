1.
A nurse. Three clipboards. A pager. A laptop. One shift.
Healthcare isn't failing from lack of data — it's drowning in it. Every visit, every wearable, every scan adds to a flood no human can fully process. The gap between data generated and data reviewed keeps growing.

2.
AI is doing what exhausted physicians often can't: finding patterns before they become crises.
Trained on millions of patient records, machine learning models are detecting early signs of heart disease, diabetes, cancer, and Alzheimer's — sometimes years before symptoms appear. Earlier signals mean wider windows to intervene.

3.
CNNs weren't built for medicine. They were built for images.
Now they're reading radiology scans and catching tumors a fatigued radiologist might miss on hour ten of a shift. The same architecture that recognizes a cat photo is helping catch arterial blockages. Repurposed tools, life-scale stakes.

4.
Your smartwatch already knows something's off before you do.
AIoT — sensors, wearables, implanted devices — feeds continuous biometric streams to models watching for drift. It's real-time monitoring at a scale no human care team can replicate. The hospital room is expanding into every wrist and pocket.

5.
Here's the unsolved part: "flag this patient" means nothing without "here's why."
Clinical workflows run on reasoning and accountability. Black-box predictions don't fit. Explainability isn't a nice-to-have — it's the barrier between an AI that assists and one that gets ignored. That's the genuinely hard problem still on the table.
